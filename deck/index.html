Process Monitoring: From Install to Trusted Coverage

Process monitoring is foundational to New Relic Infrastructure, yet under-adopted. This deck examines why adoption breaks, which operator it affects most, and a single proposed view that addresses it. The analysis is grounded in a working prototype deployed on real AWS infrastructure and instrumented with two stacks in parallel.

01 · The Frame

Detection, not resolution, is the fastest lever on MTTR.

  • Industry north star: MTTR — the metric every platform (New Relic, Datadog, Dynatrace) is sold on.
  • The chain: MTTR = MTTD (detect) + time-to-resolve. Detection requires an alert that actually fires.
  • Where the precondition breaks: process monitoring. Each host runs dozens of processes, few are watched, and alerts frequently fail silently, leaving MTTR effectively unbounded.
  • Scope: the analysis sits upstream of resolution, addressing whether detection is possible and trusted rather than incident response.

The scoped success metric

Time to First Trusted Alert (TTFTA)
The elapsed time from intent to watch a host's processes to an alert trusted to fire and reach a human.
Coverage
The percentage of running processes monitored and trusted: the "handful of dozens" gap, made measurable.

Business impact: under consumption-based pricing, adoption increases telemetry under management and account stickiness. Process samples are the highest-volume infrastructure data type, so adoption and consumption revenue move together.

03 · Scope & Assumptions

What this analysis deliberately excludes, and why.

Boundaries

  • Post-incident resolution, RCA, and triage are out of scope. The work is measured via TTFTA and coverage rather than MTTR directly.
  • The SRE incident-response journey is not modeled. The focus is onboarding through to trust.

Evidence caveats

  • The funnel is directional, not measured. Without access to New Relic activation data, the shape is grounded in observed friction while the percentages are illustrative.
  • This is an n=1 deep dive across one operator and one environment. Projections are labeled as such.

Environment

  • AWS free tier, single host. Fleet behavior is inferred, not load-tested at scale.
  • Account-specific quirks are generalized cautiously.

Findings discipline

  • Some friction was a self-inflicted configuration choice, separated from genuine product gaps. Only product gaps drive the solution.
  • The cost and revenue framing is directional and qualitative.

04 · Method

A working prototype on real infrastructure, instrumented two ways.

What was built (real data)

  • A real stack on the AWS free tier (EC2): a FastAPI service (nr-sandbox), Redis, and a background worker, with Locust generating genuine traffic.
  • Two instrumentation stacks on the same host, in parallel: New Relic (Infrastructure agent + Python APM) and open source (OpenTelemetry → Prometheus/Grafana + Tempo).
  • The deliberate failure: the worker was stopped (systemctl stop) to reproduce the "invisible until they crash" scenario.

08 · Prioritization

One fix, placed correctly, widens the funnel where the decision is made.

Funnel cliffSolution direction
Wrong pathScope disclosure at the path fork
Metrics not flowingRule-based setup health check
Not interpretableProcess→service link + surface unmonitored/critical
Alert won't fireRoute to native condition + loss-of-signal default
Nobody pagedDestination gate + rehearsal-as-backtest
Cross-cutting AINL alert creation, confirm-before-save
  • Highest impact: the interpretable cliff, where the Evaluator forms their verdict, and the literal subject of the brief ("handful of dozens" plus disconnected telemetry).
  • Best effort ratio: the data is already joined (hostname plus APM). This is a UI and entity surface, not a new pipeline.
Proposed solution, "Process Coverage": one NR Infrastructure view that connects every process to its service and host, surfaces the unmonitored and business-critical ones, and watches them in one click with a gap-safe alert configured automatically.

The remaining fixes serve as supporting work (a health check upstream; native-condition routing and a destination gate downstream), named rather than built here.

09 · The Hero Concept

Process Coverage: from a CPU-sorted list to the gaps that matter.

1

Connect

Each process row shows its service and host, navigable both ways. Closes the disconnected-telemetry gap.

2

Surface the gap

Flags unmonitored, business-critical processes by service and journey, not CPU rank. The 0.037% payment worker becomes visible.

3

Watch in one click

"Watch" auto-configures a gap-safe alert: correct attribute, loss-of-signal on, destination wired.

  • Moves both indicators: coverage up, TTFTA down, widening the funnel where the Evaluator decides.
  • Low risk: the data is already joined, so this surfaces New Relic's existing entity model rather than building a new pipeline.
AI sub-element (guardrailed): "alert me if my payment worker dies" leads NRAI to wire the correct condition, attribute, loss-of-signal, and destination, then show the configuration to confirm before saving. Nothing is saved silently.

11 · What Changes

The outcome: coverage up, time-to-trusted-alert down.

  • Addresses MTTR at its root: detection that can be trusted, configured quickly.
  • One view moves both leading indicators (coverage up, TTFTA down) and widens the funnel precisely where it is narrowest.
The Evaluator's assessment shifts from "New Relic is worse than the previous tool" to "New Relic surfaces coverage and the service map in a single view." That is the land/expand outcome the proposal targets.
Caveats. These are leading indicators, not MTTR directly (post-incident work is out of scope). The funnel is directional. The proposal relies on the entity model New Relic already has, which makes it low risk.
